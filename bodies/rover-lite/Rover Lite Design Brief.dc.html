Printed tracks
Each track is one continuous TPU 95A band, 26 mm wide and 2.8 mm thick, with inner guide teeth every 13 mm that mesh with the sprocket and run between the idler and road-wheel flanges, and outer grousers for grip. It prints standing up as a Ø140 ring with no supports and stretches into shape on the running gear. There are no pins or links to wear.
Climbing
The front idler axle sits 30 mm up, so the front run of the track meets a step face on an incline and walks up it. The rear sprocket drives, which keeps the lower run of the track in tension when climbing. The battery sits low between the tracks and puts the centre of mass 4 mm behind the hull centre, which keeps it from bucking as the nose lifts.
Neck
Pan STS3215 stands upright under a chrome turntable on a 6806 thin-section bearing. The yoke on top carries the helmet drum, which tilts about its own axis: the tilt STS3215 rides inside the drum and its horn bolts to the left yoke arm, with a 685ZZ and shoulder bolt on the right.

06b

Why these materials

Two filaments, and one of them only where flex is the point. The constraint that decided this was not strength: it was that a stock printer with no modifications should be able to make every rigid part on this body. PLA needs no hardened nozzle, no dry box, no enclosure and no 260 °C hotend, which is the difference between a pack most people can print and a pack most people can only read.

PLA
96% of the printed pieces
Everything rigid: shells, structure, brackets, bezels. Stiff, dimensionally accurate, prints unsupported on more surfaces than anything else, and stocked in every colour this body names.
TPU 95A
4% of the printed pieces
Only where compliance is the function and a rigid part would not work at all: track loop. Nothing here is TPU for toughness alone.
What PLA costs you

Snap hooks are the weak point. The snap features on this body were drawn to stay under 2.5% strain, which suits PETG. PLA's safe cantilever strain is nearer 1.5%, so the hooks are working harder than the geometry assumed. Print them with the layer lines running across the hook rather than along it, open the shell gently, and treat a hook as a part you may have to reprint. Nothing in this pack has been test-printed, so this is the first place to look for trouble and the most useful thing you can report back.

Heat. PLA's glass transition is about 55 to 60 °C, and this body's servos do not warn until 65 °C. Indoors that gap never opens: the shell sits near room temperature and the servos are the only thing that gets hot. Left in a closed car in summer, or on a sill in direct sun, the shell can soften while the electronics still consider themselves fine. That is a storage rule, not a design flaw, and it is the main thing PETG would buy back.

Heat-set inserts. Every screw lands in a brass insert, and PLA's window is narrower than PETG's: run the iron cooler, around 180 to 200 °C, go in slowly, and let the boss cool before loading it. Rushing one slumps the wall around it.

If you want the margin back: the geometry does not change. Print the shells in PETG and the load-bearing structure in CF-nylon and every part still fits, because that was the original specification and the only thing that changed is which spool is on the shelf. The cost is a hardened nozzle, a dry box, and a printer that holds 260 °C. If you have that printer, the tougher parts are yours for a filament swap.

Safety reflex · parks and dozes

{{ s.t }} {{ s.h }} {{ s.d }}
Triggers
No Central heartbeat for 500 ms · pack below 15% or 10.5 V · any servo at 70 °C or above · eFuse or DRV8833 fault · track stall over 1 s.
Always-on local reflexes
Edge: either drop ToF reads more than 25 mm past its floor baseline → brake at 2.5 m/s² and back off 30 mm. Obstacle: VL53L8CX zone under 60 mm in the direction of travel → stop. Lifted or tipped past 35° (BNO085) → tracks off.
Rest pose
Tracks stopped (clearing an edge first if one is within 40 mm). Head pan 0°, tilt −25°: the porthole looks down at the floor. Servos to 20% torque; DRV8833 asleep.
Resume
Moves again only on a fresh command from Central. Restoring the heartbeat alone does not wake it.

Verify before CAD freeze
  • Printed TPU track: tooth engagement under climbing load (no ratcheting over the sprocket), stretch over a week of tension, and whether 95A or 90A grips better on wood thresholds.
  • Track tension: set the idler slot travel (±4 mm) once the real band length is known; check the band does not walk off on pivot turns.
  • Threshold climb on a real 20 mm aluminium door strip, both directions, at an angle as well as square on.
  • Camera FFC through the pan cable clock and the tilt axis: 50,000 cycles of full pan and tilt range.
  • Drop-off ToF on dark rugs and glossy floors; the VL53L8CX can double-check edges on the forward zones.
  • N20 speed and stall at 6 V for the chosen ratio; listings vary widely between sellers.
  • Head drum packaging: tilt STS3215, display, Camera Module 3 and both mics inside Ø84 × 80, with the FFC fold behind the display.
  • Heat: Pi Zero 2 W and two bucks share the closed hull; add a vent path under the fenders if the Pi throttles.
  • Cost: decide on the savings list before ordering.
