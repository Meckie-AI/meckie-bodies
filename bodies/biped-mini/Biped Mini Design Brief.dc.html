Key finding · leg joints
Without an ankle roll joint, the flat feet cannot stay flat while it shifts its weight onto one leg.
To lift a foot, the centre of mass has to move about {{ shiftNeed }} mm sideways over the other foot. With the specified joints (hip yaw, roll, pitch, knee, ankle pitch) the only ways to do that are tilting both legs about {{ tiltNeed }}°, which tips each foot onto its outer edge, or rolling the body about 35°. This brief therefore defaults to hip roll, hip pitch, knee, ankle pitch and ankle roll per leg. To keep it near 280 mm and under 1.3 kg the two roll joints use the smaller STS3032 (6 V, on its own bus) and the pitch joints keep the STS3215. The ankle roll keeps the soles flat during the waddle. Turning in place becomes a toe-heel shuffle instead of a hip twist. Switch Leg joints in Tweaks to see the as-specified version and its check results.

Servo as structure
Every servo sits in a CF-nylon sleeve and is shimmed so its spline lands on the joint axis, then a cap closes the sleeve. The hip bracket holds the hip-pitch servo, the thigh holds the knee servo, and the ankle bracket holds the ankle-pitch servo lying flat; the shin is two CF-nylon plates and a tie bar. Every pitch joint is driven by a metal horn on the outboard side and carried by a 685ZZ on an M5 shoulder bolt on the inboard side; the roll joints use a 683ZZ on an M3 shoulder screw.
Feet
92 × 60 mm PETG plates with a 4 mm red TPU sole, set 12 mm toe-forward of the ankle axis. The two soles sit 16 mm apart, so the standing support area is 92 × 136 mm. A chrome toe cap protects the front edge.
Tail skid
A CF-nylon tail skid curves down from the back of the pelvis to a red TPU shoe 88 mm below the hip-pitch axis, behind the legs. To sit it bends the hips 66°, the knees 100° and the ankles 15°; the body tips back about 19° and the shoe lands on the floor 37 mm behind the heels, so it rests on two feet and the skid. The rear drop-off ToF sits on the skid.

06b

Why these materials

PETG
NaN% of the printed pieces
Shells and enclosures. Heat tolerance, toughness at a snap hook, and a wide enough window to take a heat-set insert without slumping the boss around it.
CF-nylon
NaN% of the printed pieces
Structure: the parts that actually carry load. Stiffness and fatigue life where a bracket or a yoke is the thing holding the body together.
TPU 95A
NaN% of the printed pieces
Anything compliant: tyres, bumpers, feet. Grip and a surface that absorbs a knock instead of transmitting it into the shell.
Why not PLA

This body's servos warn at 65 °C and cut torque at 70 °C. PLA's glass transition is about 55 to 60 °C, which is below the temperature its own thermal protection is still treating as normal. The shell would start going soft while the servos inside it were still inside their rated envelope. PETG softens around 80 to 85 °C, which puts the enclosure above the limits of what it encloses.

Impact. PLA is brittle and this body is going to hit things, and fall over while its gait or balance is being tuned. PETG yields where PLA snaps.

Snap fits. Nothing here is glued. Every shell closes on cantilever hooks and latches that flex each time it is opened, and that is the exact geometry PLA fails at. The same goes for the brass inserts every screw lands in: setting one means a 200 °C iron against the wall, and PLA's narrow window lets the boss slump where PETG holds its shape.

Where PLA is genuinely better: it is stiffer, it holds a dimension more tightly, and it prints more easily without supports. If you are making a test fit, a mock-up, or a part that never gets warm and never flexes, print it in PLA. It is the production shells that want PETG.

Safety reflex · crouches and sits on the tail skid

{{ s.t }} {{ s.h }} {{ s.d }}
Triggers
No Central heartbeat for 500 ms · pack below 15% or 10.5 V · any servo at 70 °C or above · eFuse fault · servo load spike on a non-stance leg (something caught).
Always-on local reflexes
Edge: a drop ToF sees the floor fall away → finish the step backward and stop. Obstacle: lidar under 120 mm ahead → stop. Fall: torso tilt past 35° outside the gait envelope → all servos to 10% torque so the gears survive the landing.
Rest pose
Hips 66°, knees 100°, ankles 15°, body tipped back about 19°, sitting on two feet and the tail skid. Stable with servos at 20% torque or fully off.
Resume
Stands up only on a fresh command from Central. Restoring the heartbeat alone does not wake it.

Verify before CAD freeze
  • Sign off the leg joints: ankle roll instead of hip yaw (recommended), or keep hip yaw and accept tilted feet, or go to 12 servos (+$40, about +130 g, over 1.3 kg).
  • STS3032 spline position along its 23.2 mm side is not published; every STS3032 pocket has 3 mm of shim room. Measure and shim before final assembly.
  • Roll torque margins are thin: the STS3032 ankle roll runs at about 55% of its short-term budget in single support. Keep the CoM within 8 mm of the stance foot centre line in the gait planner.
  • Collision sweep of the printed parts: hip roll ±12°, hip pitch −90° to +15°, knee 0–120°, ankle pitch ±20° (±30° once the knee is bent past 40°), ankle roll ±12°. Back-swing is limited to 15° by the tail skid.
  • Toe-heel shuffle turning on carpet and wood; measure degrees per step.
  • STS3215 backlash at the ankle (about 0.5°) magnified over the 114 mm leg; check the stance foot stays planted and does not chatter.
  • Thermal: knees and ankles during a 10 min dance at 12 V. Trip is 70 °C.
  • Sit-to-stand from the skid on a slippery floor: the feet must not skate forward as the knees extend.
  • Drop-off ToFs move with the torso pitch during the gait; correct from the IMU and test at stair tops.
  • Camera FFC from chest to head is short and fixed, but route it clear of the arm-upgrade servos.
  • Kid safety: pinch points behind the knees at full fold. Add TPU knee guards or limit the fold when a child is detected nearby.
