Servos stay low
Three of the four servos sit at or below the shoulder. Shoulder (ID 2) and elbow drive (ID 3) share one pod on the turret, splines on the shoulder axis. ID 3 drives the upper arm through a crank and pushrod that form a parallelogram with the lower arm, so ID 3 sets the upper arm's absolute angle directly and the elbow carries only a pair of bearings. Only the head-tilt servo rides at the tip.
Counterbalance
Spring A runs from a turret lug 42 mm behind and below the shoulder to a pin 60 mm up the lower arm, balancing ID 2. Spring B runs from the opposite lug to the ID 3 crank pin, balancing the upper arm and head through the pushrod. Each spring is a near-zero-length setup (low initial tension, cord-to-pin), sized to cancel about 80% of gravity torque across the working range. With power off, the arm drifts slowly instead of dropping.

Behaviour modes

Lamp moves like a character first and a light second. The two arm joints give it reach and posture, yaw turns it toward a voice, and the head tilt carries most of the expression. The eyes on the face lead each move by about 120 ms.

Snap features are sized for PLA: 1.2 mm cantilever thickness, 0.6 mm hook depth, 30° lead-in, 45° release face so they can be unsnapped with a spudger. Screws into plastic always land in a heat-set insert, never a printed thread. No visible fastener heads: every screw sits under a brass pivot cap, a chrome pod cap, the hood liner, the TPU foot or inside the base. Threadlocker is optional on metal-to-metal threads only (servo horns, shoulder bolts, rod ends); use a removable grade.

06b

Why these materials

Two filaments, and one of them only where flex is the point. The constraint that decided this was not strength: it was that a stock printer with no modifications should be able to make every rigid part on this body. PLA needs no hardened nozzle, no dry box, no enclosure and no 260 °C hotend, which is the difference between a pack most people can print and a pack most people can only read.

PLA
97% of the printed pieces
Everything rigid: shells, structure, brackets, bezels. Stiff, dimensionally accurate, prints unsupported on more surfaces than anything else, and stocked in every colour this body names.
TPU 95A
3% of the printed pieces
Only where compliance is the function and a rigid part would not work at all: foot. Nothing here is TPU for toughness alone.
What PLA costs you

Snap hooks are the weak point. The snap features on this body were drawn to stay under 2.5% strain, which suits PETG. PLA's safe cantilever strain is nearer 1.5%, so the hooks are working harder than the geometry assumed. Print them with the layer lines running across the hook rather than along it, open the shell gently, and treat a hook as a part you may have to reprint. Nothing in this pack has been test-printed, so this is the first place to look for trouble and the most useful thing you can report back.

Heat. PLA's glass transition is about 55 to 60 °C, and this body's servos do not warn until 65 °C. Indoors that gap never opens: the shell sits near room temperature and the servos are the only thing that gets hot. Left in a closed car in summer, or on a sill in direct sun, the shell can soften while the electronics still consider themselves fine. That is a storage rule, not a design flaw, and it is the main thing PETG would buy back.

Heat-set inserts. Every screw lands in a brass insert, and PLA's window is narrower than PETG's: run the iron cooler, around 180 to 200 °C, go in slowly, and let the boss cool before loading it. Rushing one slumps the wall around it.

If you want the margin back: the geometry does not change. Print the shells in PETG and the load-bearing structure in CF-nylon and every part still fits, because that was the original specification and the only thing that changed is which spool is on the shelf. The cost is a hardened nozzle, a dry box, and a printer that holds 260 °C. If you have that printer, the tougher parts are yours for a filament swap.

07

Safety reflex · folds down to sleep

{{ s.t }} {{ s.h }} {{ s.d }}
Triggers
No Central heartbeat for 500 ms · any servo at 70 °C or above · input below 13 V (charger sagging or unplugged) · eFuse fault · collision (servo current spike or position error over limit).
Asleep pose
Yaw 0°, lower arm −15°, upper arm folded to 140°, head 60° down with the hood lip just over the base front. About 260 mm tall, inside the base footprint plus 30 mm. Light dims to 10% warm.
Power loss
No battery by design. The springs hold the arm near balance, so it settles slowly onto the printed shoulder stop rather than dropping. On a collision, the move stops and torque drops to 30% for 1 s before the fold.
Resume
Wakes only on a fresh command from Central. Restoring the heartbeat alone does not wake it. The desk light can be turned back on from Central while asleep.

Verify before CAD freeze
  • Ribbon flex life through three joints: 50,000 cycles of the full shoulder, elbow and tilt range.
  • Head ESP32-S3 camera stream: latency and frame rate to Central over household Wi-Fi with the LED PWM running; antenna clear of the metal-finish hood.
  • SPI to the display at 32 MHz over 520 mm of ribbon with ground between signals; drop to 20 MHz if the face tears.
  • I²S mic lines over the same run; check for crosstalk from the LED PWM and the servo bus.
  • Spring rates: measure real gravity torque on the built arm, then pick springs that cancel 75–85% across the range. Starting points: spring A ≈ 0.13 N/mm, spring B ≈ 0.39 N/mm.
  • Pinch points at the elbow scissor and the pushrod: the rails close to 8 mm near full fold. Add a finger guard or soft-limit the fold.
  • Collision detection thresholds on STS3215 current and position feedback, so a hand in the way stops the arm.
  • PD source compatibility: 15 V at 3 A from common 45 W chargers, and behaviour on a 5 V-only charger (light only, servos disabled).
  • LED ring heat inside the hood next to the display at 100% for an hour; vent the knuckle if needed.
  • Light quality: glare onto the camera from the liner; may need a small baffle above the ring.
  • Yaw bearing and turret stiffness at full reach: tip deflection target under 2 mm.
  • Cost: price the four servos and the Pi at time of purchase; decide on the savings list before ordering.
