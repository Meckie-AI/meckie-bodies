The head is a 1950s portable television. A cream two-piece cabinet with a chrome seam band and a tapered tube-bell back carries a rectangular 2.8" IPS screen in a chrome surround, set off-centre. The eyes are drawn on the screen. A sea-foam knob column on the right holds two brass knobs and the speaker slots; a chrome carry handle finishes the top, with the camera in a chrome pod above the screen. The cabinet tilts in a brushed-aluminium-finish yoke on a two-tier sea-foam and cream swivel base with a red TPU foot. The neck harness runs up the back of the yoke in red braid. Each view can be dragged to orbit.

Servo placement
The pan servo stands upright at the pedestal centre and only supplies rotation; the 6810 bearing under the turntable carries all tipping load. The tilt servo rides inside the cabinet behind the knob column, output spline on the tilt axis, bolted through a metal horn to the left yoke arm. The cabinet pivots about its own servo, so the yoke stays a thin, clean U. The screen sits right of centre, so the CoM is about 6 mm toward the right arm; that loads the 685ZZ side slightly more but adds nothing to tilt torque.
Harness & strain relief
Six conductors leave the electronics tray, wind 1.25 turns around a printed cable clock screwed under the turntable, around the pan horn,, rise through the 6810 bore and exit at the rear of the turntable. From there the braid runs exposed up the back of the yoke in two brass P-clips and enters the head through a rear grommet with a 20 mm drip loop for tilt. Pan is soft-limited to ±150° so the clock never winds tight.

Gaze modes · look-at

Dial turns toward whoever is talking. The two mics give a coarse bearing to the voice, the pan servo swings the head there, then the camera takes over and centres the face. Eyes lead the motion on the display by about 120 ms, so the glance reads as intent instead of a mechanical swivel.

Snap features are sized for PLA: 1.2 mm cantilever thickness, 0.6 mm hook depth, 30° lead-in, 45° release face so they can be unsnapped with a spudger. Screws into plastic always land in a heat-set insert, never a printed thread. No visible fastener heads: every screw sits under the knob panel, the handle bosses, the seam band, the TPU foot or inside the battery bay. Threadlocker is optional on metal-to-metal threads only (servo horns, shoulder bolt); use a removable grade.

06b

Why these materials

Two filaments, and one of them only where flex is the point. The constraint that decided this was not strength: it was that a stock printer with no modifications should be able to make every rigid part on this body. PLA needs no hardened nozzle, no dry box, no enclosure and no 260 °C hotend, which is the difference between a pack most people can print and a pack most people can only read.

PLA
89% of the printed pieces
Everything rigid: shells, structure, brackets, bezels. Stiff, dimensionally accurate, prints unsupported on more surfaces than anything else, and stocked in every colour this body names.
TPU 95A
11% of the printed pieces
Only where compliance is the function and a rigid part would not work at all: battery strap, foot, lcd retainer (clamps the module), neck harness grommet. Nothing here is TPU for toughness alone.
What PLA costs you

Snap hooks are the weak point. The snap features on this body were drawn to stay under 2.5% strain, which suits PETG. PLA's safe cantilever strain is nearer 1.5%, so the hooks are working harder than the geometry assumed. Print them with the layer lines running across the hook rather than along it, open the shell gently, and treat a hook as a part you may have to reprint. Nothing in this pack has been test-printed, so this is the first place to look for trouble and the most useful thing you can report back.

Heat. PLA's glass transition is about 55 to 60 °C, and this body's servos do not warn until 65 °C. Indoors that gap never opens: the shell sits near room temperature and the servos are the only thing that gets hot. Left in a closed car in summer, or on a sill in direct sun, the shell can soften while the electronics still consider themselves fine. That is a storage rule, not a design flaw, and it is the main thing PETG would buy back.

Heat-set inserts. Every screw lands in a brass insert, and PLA's window is narrower than PETG's: run the iron cooler, around 180 to 200 °C, go in slowly, and let the boss cool before loading it. Rushing one slumps the wall around it.

If you want the margin back: the geometry does not change. Print the shells in PETG and the load-bearing structure in CF-nylon and every part still fits, because that was the original specification and the only thing that changed is which spool is on the shelf. The cost is a hardened nozzle, a dry box, and a printer that holds 260 °C. If you have that printer, the tougher parts are yours for a filament swap.

Verify before CAD freeze
  • STS3215 body and spline position against the Feetech drawing; the tilt spline must land on the head's CoM within ±3 mm.
  • 6810 bearing fit in a PLA seat versus a PLA insert ring; check for creep after a week of continuous scanning.
  • Cable clock fatigue: 50,000 cycles of ±150° pan on the 6-core harness and the 28 AWG signal pairs.
  • Pack fit: 3S 1500 in the right-flank bay with the hatch closed; measure the actual cell block before printing the base pan.
  • Pi Zero 2 W temperature inside the sealed head while streaming camera and mics; vent area may need to grow.
  • Mic noise from the tilt servo and Ø40 speaker inside the cabinet; add a printed TPU gasket under the mic clamp plates if needed.
  • Voice-bearing accuracy with 92 mm mic spacing (asymmetric about the pan axis; calibrate the offset); target ±15° so the pan move lands close enough for face tracking.
  • ToF window: IR-transparent material with an air gap, and its view clear of the yoke at extreme pan.
  • USB-C PD compatibility across phone, laptop and GaN chargers, plus the 5 V fallback path.
  • Shelf clamp grip on painted and laminate shelves at a 10 N push at screen-top height.
  • 2.8" LCD viewing angle when Dial looks down at a seated user; IPS needed, not TN. Check backlight bleed at the surround edges.
  • Cabinet tilt clearance: bottom edge sweeps to 14.5 mm above the yoke base at −30° and +40°; confirm with the real servo horn stack.
