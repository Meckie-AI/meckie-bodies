Layout
The two N20s lie end to end on saddles moulded into the lower-shell floor just ahead of centre, encoders facing inward, each clamped by a CF-nylon strap. The battery sits flat behind them and the ball caster at the tail, so the centre of mass falls 12 mm behind the axle: the caster carries about a fifth of the weight and Scout never rocks onto its nose.
Bump tolerance
The 11 mm red TPU bumpers, front and rear, have a flat flange clamped in the upper-shell rim and stand 5.5 mm proud of the shell. The tyres take knocks from the side. The ears and tail are TPU or spring-steel and flex out of the way. Collisions are sensed by the IMU (deceleration spike) and the encoders (stall against commanded speed), so there are no bump switches to fail.
Wheels
Printed PETG hub with a Ø3 D-bore and an M2 set screw into a radial heat-set insert · TPU 95A tyre snaps on, its inner rib locking into a groove round the hub rim · chrome hubcap with cream whitewall clicks in on three split pins

03b

Wiring diagram

VSYS / battery 3.3 V I²C Expander outputs SPI Encoders Audio / status Wi-Fi

The XIAO has 11 free header pins and Scout uses all of them. Anything slow (motor direction, driver sleep, display reset and backlight, rear ToF shutdown, amp shutdown, charger status) moves to a TCA9554 on I²C. Each DRV8833 channel takes PWM on IN1 and direction on IN2; in reverse the firmware inverts the duty. Encoders use channel A only, counted by the S3's PCNT unit, with direction taken from the command.

06b

Why these materials

PETG
62% of the printed pieces
Shells and enclosures. Heat tolerance, toughness at a snap hook, and a wide enough window to take a heat-set insert without slumping the boss around it.
CF-nylon
12% of the printed pieces
Structure: the parts that actually carry load. Stiffness and fatigue life where a bracket or a yoke is the thing holding the body together.
TPU 95A
27% of the printed pieces
Anything compliant: tyres, bumpers, feet. Grip and a surface that absorbs a knock instead of transmitting it into the shell.
Why not PLA

PLA's glass transition is about 55 to 60 °C. A closed shell over a motor driver and a battery, on a desk in a sunny window or carried in a car, reaches that without trying. PETG softens around 80 to 85 °C, so the enclosure stays ahead of what it encloses.

Impact. PLA is brittle and this body is going to hit things, and fall over while its gait or balance is being tuned. PETG yields where PLA snaps.

Snap fits. Nothing here is glued. Every shell closes on cantilever hooks and latches that flex each time it is opened, and that is the exact geometry PLA fails at. The same goes for the brass inserts every screw lands in: setting one means a 200 °C iron against the wall, and PLA's narrow window lets the boss slump where PETG holds its shape.

Where PLA is genuinely better: it is stiffer, it holds a dimension more tightly, and it prints more easily without supports. If you are making a test fit, a mock-up, or a part that never gets warm and never flexes, print it in PLA. It is the production shells that want PETG.

07

Safety reflex · sits and naps

{{ s.t }} {{ s.h }} {{ s.d }}
Triggers
No Central heartbeat for 500 ms · battery below 15% or 3.45 V · motor driver fault (nFAULT) or stall over 1 s · motor current over limit.
Always-on local reflexes
Edge: either ToF reads more than 25 mm past its floor baseline → brake at 3.5 m/s², then back off 20 mm. Lift: IMU free-fall or both ToF out of range → motors off at once. These run on the S3 even with Central connected.
Rest pose
Scout has no joints, so resting means stopping where it is, unless a ToF sees an edge within 40 mm, in which case it first creeps away from it at 0.05 m/s. Motors to coast with the driver asleep.
Resume
Wakes only on a fresh command from Central. Restoring the heartbeat alone does not wake it. Being picked up and set down again does not wake it either.

Verify before CAD freeze
  • Drop-off ToF on dark rugs, glossy tables and glass: VL53L0X can read black carpet as "no floor". Tune the baseline per surface and test stair edges and table edges at full speed.
  • Front ToF window under the bumper: check the bumper does not enter the 25° cone at any bump deflection.
  • Electronics deck rear screws land in notches at the tub's rounded corners; confirm the heads clamp firmly.
  • Real N20 speed and stall torque at 3.5 V for the chosen ratio; listings vary widely between sellers.
  • Encoder channel A only: confirm odometry drift is acceptable for Central's navigation, or add channel B by moving the lidar XSHUT onto a second expander.
  • Wi-Fi range with the tail whip inside a cream PETG dome and the antenna lead routed past the motors.
  • PDM-to-PAM8302 audio quality through an RC filter; if it is too noisy, swap to a MAX98357A and free a pin by moving the display DC to an expander-driven 9-bit SPI mode.
  • IMU choice: the shared core calls for a BNO085 at the centre of mass. Scout uses an LSM6DSOX to hold the budget; confirm heading drift is acceptable or take the +$15.
  • Ears and tail: TPU ears must not reach the wheels when floppy; the whip must spring back after being stepped on.
  • Shell scuffing: chrome-effect paint on the bezel and hood will chip on bumps; consider printed silver PETG for those parts.
