Concept views

A wide, squared-off body like a 1950s table radio, 120 mm across and only 74 mm deep, with a sea-foam belly tub and a cream upper split by two chrome bands. It rides on two red TPU wheels with cream hubs and chrome caps, set wide at 136 mm. The face sits in a chrome-bezelled collar, with the camera in a chrome hood above it. Red TPU bumpers round off both top corners, and two spring antennae with red balls bob as it sways. Two brushed-finish kickstand legs with red feet fold flat against the belly front and back. The head harness runs up the back in red braid. Each view can be dragged to orbit.

Balance stack
The two N20s lie end to end on the axle line in a PLA motor block, encoders facing inward. The BNO085 stands on a bracket between the two encoders, on the axle line itself, so it reads pitch with no lever-arm acceleration from wheel moves. The ESP32-S3 runs the balance loop at 400 Hz from the IMU's 400 Hz gyro and game-rotation reports.
Battery high
The 2S pack sits behind the face, {{ battYStr }} mm above the floor. That lifts the centre of mass to {{ comAxleStr }} mm above the axle, which slows the fall (time constant {{ tauStr }} ms) and gives the loop more time per correction. The torque needed to hold a lean does not change with height, so the motors are not penalised.
Kickstands
One MG90S micro servo in the belly drives both legs through two pushrods. Each leg swings 118° from flat against the shell to down-and-out, and the linkage goes 4° over centre at full deploy, so the robot's weight locks the legs open instead of loading the servo. Feet land 70 mm ahead of and behind the axle.

Wobble's body language comes from the balance setpoint. Shifting the target pitch makes it lean, rock or bob; the balance loop does the rest and keeps it upright.

Balance, edge & load check

Motors: {{ motorName }}, duty capped so they see at most 6.5 V from the 2S pack. Robot {{ totalStr }} g with the centre of mass {{ comAxleStr }} mm above the axle; wheel radius 32 mm. Transient budget is 60% of stall per motor, continuous 30%. Reflected rotor inertia through the gearbox is added to the robot's effective mass. A balancing robot cannot brake straight away: it must first roll forward to lean back, modelled as 250 ms plus 30 ms reaction, then decelerate at 2 m/s².

Recoverable lean is the angle at which the motors can still drive the wheels under the centre of mass faster than it falls (base acceleration above g·tanθ). 15° covers the ±8° talking sway plus a light nudge. 06b

Why these materials

Two filaments, and one of them only where flex is the point. The constraint that decided this was not strength: it was that a stock printer with no modifications should be able to make every rigid part on this body. PLA needs no hardened nozzle, no dry box, no enclosure and no 260 °C hotend, which is the difference between a pack most people can print and a pack most people can only read.

PLA
79% of the printed pieces
Everything rigid: shells, structure, brackets, bezels. Stiff, dimensionally accurate, prints unsupported on more surfaces than anything else, and stocked in every colour this body names.
TPU 95A
21% of the printed pieces
Only where compliance is the function and a rigid part would not work at all: antenna ball, corner bumper, kickstand foot, tyre. Nothing here is TPU for toughness alone.
What PLA costs you

Snap hooks are the weak point. The snap features on this body were drawn to stay under 2.5% strain, which suits PETG. PLA's safe cantilever strain is nearer 1.5%, so the hooks are working harder than the geometry assumed. Print them with the layer lines running across the hook rather than along it, open the shell gently, and treat a hook as a part you may have to reprint. Nothing in this pack has been test-printed, so this is the first place to look for trouble and the most useful thing you can report back.

Heat. PLA's glass transition is about 55 to 60 °C. Indoors that is never approached. Left in a closed car in summer, or on a sill in direct sun, the shell can soften. That is a storage rule, not a design flaw, and it is the main thing PETG would buy back.

Heat-set inserts. Every screw lands in a brass insert, and PLA's window is narrower than PETG's: run the iron cooler, around 180 to 200 °C, go in slowly, and let the boss cool before loading it. Rushing one slumps the wall around it.

If you want the margin back: the geometry does not change. Print the shells in PETG and the load-bearing structure in CF-nylon and every part still fits, because that was the original specification and the only thing that changed is which spool is on the shelf. The cost is a hardened nozzle, a dry box, and a printer that holds 260 °C. If you have that printer, the tougher parts are yours for a filament swap.

Safety reflex · rolls to a stop and sits

{{ s.t }} {{ s.h }} {{ s.d }}
Triggers
No Central heartbeat for 500 ms · pack below 20% or 6.8 V (sits with margin to spare) · DRV8833 fault · motor stall over 1 s · XIAO link lost for 2 s.
Always-on local reflexes
Edge: a drop ToF reads more than 25 mm past its pitch-corrected floor distance → stop and back off 40 mm. Obstacle: lidar under 200 mm → stop. Fall: pitch past 35° → motors off at once, oops face.
Rest pose
Upright on both wheels and both kickstand feet, legs locked over centre, motors off, servo unpowered. Stable to 36° of push before it would tip over a foot.
Resume
Stands up only on a fresh command from Central: balance loop engages on the legs, then the legs fold away. Restoring the heartbeat alone does not wake it.

Verify before CAD freeze
  • N20 gearbox backlash: measure it on the chosen motors. More than about 2° at the wheel makes the balance loop buzz; the N30 or a different gearbox may be needed even if torque passes.
  • Balance tuning on real hardware with the pack high; confirm the CoM height and trim the pitch offset.
  • Drop-off ToF with pitch correction while swaying: the floor reading moves several mm with each rock. Test table edges at full cruise speed.
  • Kickstand over-centre lock: check it holds with the robot pushed 20° and that the MG90S can unlock it from the resting load.
  • Total power loss means a fall from 200 mm. Drop-test the corner bumpers and face bezel onto a hard floor and check the display survives.
  • Motor overvolt: N20 6 V motors on 2S with the duty capped; check winding temperature after 30 min of balancing.
  • XIAO pins: all 11 are used. Confirm the camera, SPI and both I²S directions coexist without conflicts.
  • IMU noise from motor PWM and magnet fields at the axle; the BNO085 game-rotation vector ignores the magnetometer, but check gyro noise.
  • Shared-core deviations to sign off: micro servo instead of STS3215, 2S instead of 3S (DRV8833 is limited to 10.8 V), XIAO Sense instead of Pi Zero 2 W with Camera Module 3.
